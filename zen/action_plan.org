* Overview

This document tracks all issues identified in the Smolin/Rovelli/Hossenfelder critical review of
docs 1–5 (zen series). Items are classified as *TEXT FIX* (correcting a claim that
overstates what has been shown) or *OPEN MATH* (the claim cannot be fixed without
doing new work). A text fix without the math behind it is not a resolution — it is
honest acknowledgment.

Severity levels: FATAL (must resolve before any submission) / SEVERE / SERIOUS / MODERATE.

* FATAL Issues

** FATAL [F1] G variation prediction may already be observationally ruled out
:PROPERTIES:

:TYPE: OPEN MATH, then TEXT FIX
:END:

The framework predicts dG/dt = −2G·H₀, giving Ġ/G ≈ −1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰/yr.
Current observational bounds (MESSENGER, LLR, pulsar timing) constrain |Ġ/G| < 4–9 × 10⁻¹³/yr —
roughly 200× tighter than the prediction.

*** Action steps

1. [ ] Run the numbers precisely: compute |Ġ/G| = 2H₀ and compare against the most
   current published bounds (Genova et al. 2018 from MESSENGER gives
   Ġ/G = (0.1 ± 1.4) × 10⁻¹³/yr).
2. [ ] If ruled out: remove the prediction from the predbox in Doc 5 and replace with
   an explicit acknowledgment that the framework's G-variation prediction is
   inconsistent with current solar system ephemeris data, and discuss what
   modification would suppress it (e.g., G variation only over Hubble timescales,
   not at the current epoch).
3. [ ] If salvageable: identify the physical mechanism that would suppress local
   variation while allowing secular variation — this likely requires the floor
   condition derivation (see MODERATE-5). Document what additional assumptions
   are needed.
4. [ ] Apply the same check to dm_p/dt = m_p · H₀/3: atomic clock comparisons and
   quasar spectroscopy constrain |ṁ_p/m_p| at the < 10⁻¹⁴/yr level. The
   predicted rate is ~7 × 10⁻¹¹/yr. This may also be ruled out.

*** Text fix (contingent on step 1 outcome)

  an explicit "tension with current observational bounds" callout.

** FATAL [F2] SU(3) symmetry claim is mathematically incorrect as stated
:PROPERTIES:
:DOC: 3
:TYPE: OPEN MATH + TEXT FIX
:END:

Doc 3 states: "The continuous symmetry group preserving L is SU(3)."

This is wrong as written. The Laplacian of K₃ has eigenvalues {0, 3, 3}. Any unitary
U that preserves N†LN must preserve the eigenspaces of L: the one-dimensional
λ=0 eigenspace (span of (1,1,1)/√3) and the two-dimensional λ=3 eigenspace.
The group of such unitaries is U(1) × U(2), not SU(3). SU(3) would require L ∝ I,
which holds only for the trivial graph.

*** Action steps

1. [ ] Compute explicitly the symmetry group of N†LN for K₃ with L = 2I − A.
   Show that it is U(1) × U(2) (or its SU-subgroup SU(1) × SU(2) ≅ SU(2)).
2. [ ] Identify what additional structure beyond the Laplacian quadratic form could
   give SU(3). Candidates:
   a. A different invariant constructed from N and the graph structure (e.g., a
      cubic invariant using the antisymmetric tensor ε_{ijk}).
   b. Restricting to the λ=3 eigenspace and looking at internal symmetries there.
   c. Treating N ∈ ℂ³ as a fundamental representation and asking what the
      automorphisms of the triangle structure are when edges carry complex phases.
3. [ ] If SU(3) cannot be recovered: replace the SU(3) claim with what is actually
   true (U(1) × U(2) or similar), and demote the strong force section to a
   structural hypothesis.

*** Text fix (do immediately regardless)

In Doc 3 §Symmetries, replace the plainbox claim "the continuous symmetry group
preserving L is SU(3)" with the correct group. The paragraph below it already has
the right hedging ("Whether it is precisely SU(3)…is an open derivation") — the
plainbox incorrectly states it as established. Sync the plainbox to match the
body.

** SEVERE [S2] Fermionic statistics — proton cannot be described by this Lagrangian
:PROPERTIES:
:DOC: 3, 4
:TYPE: OPEN MATH
:END:

The negotiation Lagrangian is bosonic. Canonical quantization gives
[â, â†] = 1 (Bose-Einstein statistics). The proton is a spin-½ fermion obeying
Fermi-Dirac statistics and the Pauli exclusion principle. The framework does not
describe the particle it claims to derive.

Two candidate resolutions are named in the docs but neither is developed:
(a) Grassmann-valued N, or (b) composite antisymmetrization.

*** Action steps

1. [ ] Investigate option (b) first, as it stays within the existing framework:
   Show whether three bosonic quanta occupying the three distinguishable nodes of
   K₃ with the antisymmetric combination |r⟩|g⟩|b⟩ − |r⟩|b⟩|g⟩ + ... behave as
   a fermion under exchange of two protons. This is analogous to how the
   color-singlet combination of three quarks gives a fermionic hadron despite the
   individual color degrees of freedom being bosonic in some formulations.
2. [ ] If (b) works: document the explicit antisymmetric state, show the exchange
   statistics, and update Doc 3 and Doc 4 to present this as the resolution.
3. [ ] If (b) fails: adopt option (a) — replace N with a Grassmann-valued field,
   rederive the Lagrangian, and show the anticommutation relations follow.

*** Text fix (do immediately)

Both Doc 3 and Doc 4 correctly flag this as an open problem. The Doc 4 summary
list (item 2: "Proton spin-up and spin-down are degenerate eigenstates") should
