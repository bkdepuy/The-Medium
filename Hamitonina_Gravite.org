* Summary

The Hamiltonian threshold and the quantum vacuum minimum assumed in the G
derivation are the same substrate condition. G and the Hamiltonian are
co-emergent. They do not appear separately. They crystallize together at the
vacuum floor. This session developed that claim into separable nodes.

* Nodes

** Hamiltonian — domain of validity
:PROPERTIES:
:STATUS: proposed
:END:

The Hamiltonian is not defined at the substrate level. It emerges at the
minimum iota-interaction threshold sufficient to produce a stable,
metrically-embedded, temporally-ordered manifestation in the projection layer.
Below this threshold the concepts of kinetic energy, potential energy, and
energy conservation are undefined. The Hamiltonian has a floor. Below the
floor it does not exist — not approximately, not formally. It simply does not
apply.

** Iota interaction — pre-energetic character
:PROPERTIES:
:STATUS: proposed
:END:

Iota interaction at the substrate level is neither kinetic nor potential
energy. It is a pre-metric coupling — boundary negotiation geometry, graded
coupling intensity ξ — that is the common ancestor of both categories. Kinetic
and potential are the names the projection layer gives to two aspects of what
was always one thing at the substrate. The distinction between them is
emergent, not primitive.

** Hamiltonian threshold and vacuum minimum — same event
:PROPERTIES:
:STATUS: proposed
:CONNECTS: G-derivation, hamiltonian-threshold
:END:

The threshold at which the Hamiltonian becomes well-defined and the quantum
vacuum minimum assumed in the G derivation are the same substrate condition
approached from two directions. The G derivation requires a minimum
iota-interaction density — the vacuum floor at 5.96 × 10⁻²⁷ kg/m³ — before G
is a meaningful ratio. The Hamiltonian requires the same minimum before energy,
time, and conservation laws are meaningful. G and H are co-emergent. They do
not appear separately. They crystallize together at the vacuum minimum.

** Planck scale as phase boundary
:PROPERTIES:
:STATUS: proposed
:END:

The Planck scale is not merely a convenient unit system. It is the dimensional
signature of the phase boundary between substrate and projection. Below Planck
scale: raw negotiation, no conserved quantities, no metric, no time, no G, no
Hamiltonian. Above Planck scale: emergent physics, all quantities mutually
consistent, all co-emergent from the same threshold event. Planck units mark
where the plenum becomes legible as physics.

** Vacuum energy catastrophe — Hamiltonian applied below its floor
:PROPERTIES:
:STATUS: proposed
:CONNECTS: cosmological-constant-wrong-problem, depth-of-the-medium
:END:

The 10¹²³ gap between the Planck-scale vacuum energy prediction and the
observed vacuum energy density is not a failed calculation. It is the result of
applying a Hamiltonian formalism below its domain of validity. The Hamiltonian
does not exist at the Planck ceiling. It becomes well-defined only at the
vacuum floor. The gap is the depth of the medium between those two boundaries.
Reframing the cosmological constant problem as the wrong problem and reframing
the Hamiltonian as having a threshold are the same move.

** Constants as co-emergent package
:PROPERTIES:
:STATUS: open
:NOTE: requires formal development
:END:

G, H, and the conservation laws they imply are not independent quantities that
happen to coexist. They are a package. They acquire their values together at
the vacuum minimum threshold, as a single geometric event in the plenum. The
constants are not free parameters tuned separately. They are the signature of
one threshold condition expressed in multiple dimensions. This is why changing
one changes the others — they are facets of the same crystallization.
